### Scientific clarifications

- The ISA1/ISA2 complex supports amylopectin biosynthesis. ISA3 and limit-dextrinase/pullulanase-type activity contribute to leaf starch breakdown. A shared debranching chemistry does not assign identical physiological roles to all isoforms. The old maps’ `isa1/2` label in the breakdown pathway has therefore been qualified. See [Streb et al., 2012, The Simultaneous Abolition of Three Starch Hydrolases Blocks Transient Starch Breakdown in Arabidopsis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3516724/).
- A GBSS defect principally affects amylose, and does not make a plant automatically starchless. Iodine colour can change with polymer composition. See [Natural Polymorphisms in Arabidopsis Result in Wide Variation or Loss of the Amylose Component of Starch](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6997676/).
- Amylose is predominantly linear; sparse branching can occur in real preparations.
- TLC co-migration supports provisional identification, while quantitative claims need calibration. Glucose is a monosaccharide, not a polymer. The small-sugar detection step includes acid treatment and heating, rather than heating alone.
- The references to `bam1`, `isa1/isa2` and `sex4` remain a historical theory question. No Arabidopsis wet-lab session is included in this day.

The four questions are adapted into English inline. The approximate 46% potato and 43% maize β-amylolytic limits are given values from the assignment. They are not generated or independently verified experimental measurements in this workbook.

The folded discussion preserves the intended answers while qualifying two points: the percentage does not uniquely measure branch density, and debranching does not guarantee 100% maltose for every chain-length distribution or enzyme preparation. The original PDFs retain their historical wording. The distinction between α(1→4) substrate linkages and the initial β configuration of released maltose follows [IUBMB EC 3.2.1.2](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/enzyme/EC3/2/1/2.html).
